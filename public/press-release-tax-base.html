Hubbard County’s Growing Tax Base Provides a Strong Foundation for Responsible Budget Management

For Immediate Release - October 2026

HUBBARD COUNTY, MINN. — In an uncertain economic environment marked by inflationary pressures, rising service demands, and shifting state and federal funding responsibilities, responsible county budget management requires looking beyond a single year’s budget or property tax levy. It means evaluating the broader financial picture, including population growth, employment, tax capacity, revenue trends, and the cost of delivering essential public services.

Recent economic and financial data provide encouraging evidence that Hubbard County’s expanding tax base has helped moderate the property tax rate associated with county operations. While financial challenges remain, these trends offer important context for evaluating the county’s approach to managing taxpayer resources.

Economic Growth Strengthens Hubbard County

According to population estimates from the U.S. Census Bureau, Hubbard County’s population grew approximately 3.3% between 2020 and 2024, adding 706 residents. That was more than twice Minnesota’s statewide growth rate of approximately 1.5% during the same period.

The county has also experienced positive employment growth. According to Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development (DEED) figures previously reviewed, Hubbard County added 469 jobs between 2019 and 2024, representing employment growth of 8.1%. This performance placed Hubbard County 23rd among Minnesota’s 87 counties for employment growth.

Population and employment growth are important indicators of a healthy local economy. They can expand the property tax base, support local businesses, and create opportunities for communities. At the same time, growth can increase demand for roads, public safety, and other county services, making responsible financial planning especially important.

Tax Capacity Has Expanded Significantly

One of the most important measures of a county’s financial position is its net tax capacity, which reflects the taxable capacity of its property tax base.

Hubbard County’s net tax capacity was approximately $36.37 million in 2019. The Minnesota Department of Revenue’s final 2025 report lists the county’s net tax capacity at approximately $74.47 million.

That represents an increase of approximately $38.10 million, or 104.8%, between 2019 and 2025. In other words, the county’s net tax capacity more than doubled over six years.

This expansion is significant because a growing tax base provides a broader foundation for funding public services. When tax capacity grows faster than the county levy, the county can generally maintain a lower tax rate than it otherwise would need, assuming other factors remain equal.

Levy Growth Has Been Substantially Slower Than Tax Capacity Growth

Hubbard County’s final property tax levy increased from approximately $15.01 million in 2019 to $21.4 million in 2025. That represents an increase of approximately $6.39 million, or 42.6%.

Over the same period, net tax capacity increased approximately 104.8%.

This is an important distinction: Hubbard County’s net tax capacity more than doubled, while the county levy increased by less than half that percentage.

The county’s expanding tax base has helped moderate the tax rate needed to support county operations. This provides useful context when evaluating levy increases and demonstrates why budget management should consider both revenue growth and expenditure decisions.

What Does This Mean for Property Owners?

A declining county tax rate can help moderate the county portion of property taxes. However, an individual property owner’s total tax bill depends on several factors, including changes in assessed property value, property classification, and levies imposed by other taxing jurisdictions.

For that reason, a declining county tax rate does not guarantee that every property owner’s total tax bill will decrease. Nevertheless, an expanding tax base can help reduce the pressure on the county taxpayers.

The key point is that responsible budget management involves evaluating both spending and revenue. Tax capacity growth is not a substitute for expenditure control, but it is an important part of understanding the county’s overall financial position.

Meeting Today’s Challenges While Planning for Tomorrow

Like counties throughout Minnesota, Hubbard County faces difficult budget decisions. Inflation, public safety expenses, road and bridge maintenance, increasing service demands, and state and federal mandates or cost shifts can all place pressure on county finances.

These challenges require county leaders to carefully prioritize spending, identify sustainable revenue sources, and make decisions that balance immediate needs with long-term financial stability.

Responsible management does not mean avoiding every increase in spending or taxation. It means understanding the reasons behind those increases, scrutinizing expenditures, pursuing opportunities to strengthen the local economy, and ensuring taxpayer dollars are used effectively.

The Bottom Line

Hubbard County’s population and employment growth, combined with the significant expansion of its property tax base, provide a positive foundation for financial management.

From 2019 through 2025, the county’s net tax capacity increased approximately 104.8%, while its final property tax levy increased approximately 42.6%. These figures indicate that the county’s tax base has grown substantially faster than the levy, helping moderate the tax rate needed to fund county services. Current numbers and projections indicate 2026 and 2027 will follow the same trend.

In an uncertain economic environment, the county must continue balancing fiscal responsibility with the needs of public safety, roads and infrastructure, and the communities it serves.

A sound county budget is about more than how much government spends. It is also about how effectively the county manages its revenue, strengthens its tax base, and prepares for future obligations. Hubbard County’s expanding tax base is an encouraging sign, and continued responsible financial management will be essential to building on that progress.
